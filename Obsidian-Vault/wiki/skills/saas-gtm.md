# SaaS GTM

Chapter 2 of the [[gtm-agent-machine|GTM agent machine]] focuses on indie SaaS — selling access to software. The core job is getting the right people to see the product do the thing every week, plus a "money loop" that keeps the ones who already paid. This page documents the four SaaS-specific workflows, each implementing the [[four-part-workflow-pattern|four-part workflow pattern]].

## Key Ideas

### Demo-First Content

- **Process**: Every time a new use case is shipped, record the product doing one specific job for one specific kind of user. Give the agent that recording plus the questions people ask under your posts.
- **Output**: Agent drafts the demo post, then a run of daily short posts for platforms where buyers already read. Each post is written from what the last ones taught.
- **Manual task placed on your list**: Make the first outputs by hand for people who commented — "because a stranger who got a personalized result tells someone."
- **Approval gate**: Every post waits in the channel for edit and approval.
- **Launch positioning**: A launch is "one beat inside this workflow rather than the plan." A launch on several surfaces buys a day of attention, some backlinks, and some feedback, but the real work is the audience built before it and the follow-up to everyone who tried the product after it.

### Competitor Placements

- **Premise**: Competitors have already found every page that sends them customers. Category pages (that list multiple competitors) will list you too if you ask.
- **Process**: Pick a keyword you want to own. Agent takes top results, pulls pages that link to the products already ranking, keeps only domains that link to several competitors (those are roundups, resource pages, and directories).
- **Output**: A placement sheet with one row per page: what they list, what a better entry would say, who maintains it, and a drafted ask to either add you or swap their entry for your more complete one.
- **Delivery**: You send every ask yourself — "because a maintainer answers a person and deletes a script."
- **Caution on directories**: Submit only where the listing shows a real link. Treat the whole batch as "an afternoon of housekeeping rather than a traffic channel."

### Warm Outbound for SaaS

- **Difference from agency version**: Same setup but with one change in the source. Next to post engagers, add the followers of accounts your buyers already trust.
- **Process**: Agent matches all against your profile, keeps the fit, drafts a message referencing what they engaged with, hands you a tracked list with a small weekly cap on how many people you touch.
- **Value of the cap**: A run with no replies is a message or profile problem. Sending more of it teaches you nothing.

### The Money Loop

- **Priority**: "The workflow everyone sets up last and should set up first" — the only one that makes revenue from customers you already have.
- **Trigger**: Payment event — a failed charge or cancellation — coming from Stripe or whatever processor, joined with the customer's history from the CRM.
- **Failed charge handling**: Gets a retry timed by the failure reason plus a short email.
- **Cancellation handling**: Gets a short exit survey, then a win-back message written for the reason they gave, sent within a day or two.
- **Churn list**: Every event writes one row to a churn list split into "card failed" and "chose to leave" — so the two get fixed differently.
- **Approval gate**: Emails and win-back messages wait for your approval.
- **Revenue claim**: "Recovered revenue is the cheapest revenue in the whole system."
- **Recommended first workflow**: Start with the money loop if there are paying customers; start with demo-first content if there are not.
