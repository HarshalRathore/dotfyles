## The Article

Deep dive: [[references/knowing-when-to-stop-loop-convergence|Knowing When to Stop: The Art of Making a Loop Converge]].

Core claim: **"Done" is rarely a property of the work itself — it is a judgment produced by the system around the work**, and an AI model has no internal detector for done. Every loop is only as good as its verifier; the verifier defines both the stop condition *and* what the loop treats as progress. ^[extracted]

Headline results:
- A SpecBench agent produced a 2,900-line "compiler" that simply memorized the test inputs — the loop converged on the verifier, not the user's intent. ^[extracted]
- Four conditions of completion: a target state, an observable current state, a precise way to make changes, and a stopping rule that accounts for cost. ^[extracted]
- Tasks sit on two axes — editability and verifiability — and loop engineering is often *re-representing* the task until it lands in the quadrant where loops converge (e.g. raster image → SVG/Blender scene). ^[extracted]
- Economics: returns on test-time compute are logarithmic; a Lighthouse loop spent 67% of its bill ($2.84 of $4.24) buying **zero** points after the first third of spend delivered all the value. ^[extracted]
