# How to Speak and Five Financial Equations: Lumen's X Post

## Overview

Lumen's August 1, 2026 X post uses a short, dramatic frame for Patrick Henry Winston's MIT lecture **How to Speak** and embeds a roughly 62-minute copy of the January 2018 lecture. The post also quotes Lumen's separate X Article, **The Five Equations That Quietly Run Your Entire Financial Life**, so the source contains two independent knowledge threads rather than one unified argument. ^[extracted]

The lecture itself is corroborated by MIT OpenCourseWare, which identifies the instructor, January IAP 2018 course, video resource, chapter outline, and downloadable transcript. ^[extracted]

## Distilled Threads

### Speaking and presentation

Winston's lecture treats speaking as a learnable repertoire: knowledge and practice can outweigh natural talent; a talk should begin with an empowerment promise; ideas need cycling, fences, verbal punctuation, and well-chosen questions; and the room, board, props, and slides are part of the argument. ^[extracted]

The reusable synthesis is [[concepts/technical-presentation|technical presentation]]: make the audience know why it is there, keep attention on the speaker and idea, situate work in a problem, show what has been done, and end on contributions rather than a generic thank-you. ^[inferred]

### Financial equations

The quoted X Article organizes five standard formulas around compound growth, doubling time, present value, geometric mean, and inflation-adjusted return. The article's practical aim is to make financial claims inspectable instead of accepting nominal figures or advertised averages at face value. ^[extracted]

The formulas and their caveats are distilled in [[concepts/financial-equations|Financial Equations]]. The article's claims about how much these equations explain, the wealth impact of fees, and the superiority of particular financial choices are rhetorical or assumption-dependent and remain marked as ambiguous. ^[ambiguous]
