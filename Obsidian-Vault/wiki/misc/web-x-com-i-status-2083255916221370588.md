# Andrew Ng Agentic AI Course: X Clip, Official Course, and Graph-Engineering Article

> **Source:** [Roan's X post](https://x.com/i/status/2083255916221370588), retrieved through a Nitter mirror because direct X retrieval was blocked. The post embeds a video and links [How to Use Graph Engineering to Build a Multi-Factor Alpha Model](https://nitter.tiekoetter.com/i/article/2080296261576687751). ^[extracted]

## What the Post Says

Roan recommends Andrew Ng's Agentic AI course as a route into agent engineering and labels the embedded clip as a free “1 hour course.” The post's chapter labels are: agent basics, agentic workflows and design patterns, practical tips, self-improving agent loops, and multi-agent systems. Its claim that the course can lead to an Anthropic job in weeks is personal encouragement, not a verifiable outcome. ^[extracted] ^[ambiguous]

## Source and Media Check

The embedded MP4 is 6,351.424 seconds long (1:45:51.424), not one hour. It contains H.264 video and 48 kHz stereo AAC audio. The temporary local copy was normalized to 16 kHz mono signed-16-bit PCM WAV and transcribed through BharatCode's remote `whisper-large-v3-turbo` endpoint in 106 bounded windows of at most 60 seconds. The resulting timing is approximate because the service returned text-only payloads rather than complete segment metadata. ^[extracted]

The captured media is a selected course excerpt, not the complete DeepLearning.AI course. Its transcript covers the introductory agentic-workflow material, practical evaluation and optimization, planning, and multi-agent communication. It does not provide the full reflection and tool-use lessons listed in the official course outline. ^[extracted]

The caption's chapter labels are directionally useful but not exact: the 53:27 marker lands in the practical error-analysis section, the 1:20:30 marker lands near planning, and the 1:30:19 marker lands inside the multi-agent section. The clip's measured duration and the official course page's 9h55m course duration both contradict treating the post as a complete one-hour course. ^[extracted] ^[ambiguous]

## Linked Graph-Engineering Article

The linked article proposes a progression from prompts to scheduled loops, parallel swarms, and finally graphs whose nodes and data edges encode coordination. It applies that framing to an eleven-node multi-factor investing workflow: seven factor-construction agents in parallel, followed by validation, regime auditing, portfolio construction, and risk decomposition. ^[extracted]

The article presents Slate, a terminal-based runtime from Random Labs, as the implementation vehicle and reports claims about model selection, daily execution, budgets, and trading signals. Those product, cost, throughput, and financial-performance claims are self-reported and were not independently verified. The article's advice is preserved as source material, not as a recommendation to install software or trade. ^[ambiguous]
